# Academic Paper: Seasonal Variability in Global Industrial Fishing Effort

**Source:** Guiet et al. (2019), PLOS ONE
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0216819
**Type:** Peer-reviewed academic paper using GFW data

## Summary

First detailed global analysis of seasonal variability in fishing effort using GFW AIS data (2015–2017), differentiated by gear type.

## Key Findings

1. **The strongest seasonal signal is China's Summer Fishing Moratorium** (May–September), which creates a massive reduction in globally observed effort during boreal summer.

2. **After removing the Chinese EEZ, global fishing effort is remarkably constant throughout the year** for most gear types — the variation is only ±15% for trawlers, longlines, fixed gear, and purse seiners.

3. **Two gear types show strong seasonality:**
   - Coastal purse seiners (±50% variation — tied to seasonal fish migrations)
   - Squid jiggers (±50% variation — tied to squid biomass production cycles)

4. **Holiday effects:** Boreal winter dips correspond to Christmas (~4 lost days in December), New Year (~4 days in January), and Chinese New Year (~2.5 days in February).

5. **Spatial seasonality is more important than total effort seasonality:** High-seas longliners and purse seiners maintain constant total deployment but shift *where* they fish dramatically across seasons (following tuna migrations, squid aggregations).

6. **Trawlers are the most temporally stable** — they fish the same areas year-round with minimal variation, targeting multiple species to buffer against seasonal changes.

## Relevance to Our Chart (Sheet 3: Monthly Fishing Trend)

This paper is the academic confirmation of what we see in our trend line:

- **The V-shaped dips every May–August in China (blue line)** = Summer Fishing Moratorium. The paper explicitly identifies this as the dominant seasonal signal in all global AIS data.
- **The smaller dips in December–February across all lines** = Holiday effects (Christmas, New Year, Chinese New Year).
- **The relative flatness of non-Chinese lines (orange, green, yellow)** = Paper confirms most gear types outside China maintain constant effort year-round.
- **The overall upward trend 2020–2024** is not covered by this paper (2015–2017 data) but our data shows the post-COVID expansion, driven by China's subsidy-fueled fleet growth.

## Citable Quote (paraphrased for compliance)

The paper finds that societal controls (regulations and holidays) dominate total fishing effort variability, while ecological drivers (fish behavior, migration) primarily affect where fishing occurs rather than how much.
