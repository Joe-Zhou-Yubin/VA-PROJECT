# EJF: "Out of Sight, Out of Control" — Global Squid Fleets Investigation (2026)

**Source:** https://ejfoundation.org/reports/global-squid-report
**Published:** June 2026
**Type:** NGO investigative report (5 years of research, 430+ interviews)

## Summary

The Environmental Justice Foundation published one of the most comprehensive investigations into the global squid industry, exposing systemic illegal fishing, environmental destruction, and severe human rights abuses across three major fisheries:

- **Northwest Indian Ocean (NWIO)**
- **Southeast Pacific (SEP)**
- **Southwest Atlantic (SWA)**

These three regions supply approximately **60% of the world's squid**.

## Key Findings

- **430+ interviews** with Indonesian and Filipino fishers working aboard **249 distant-water vessels**
- Nearly all interviewees reported **at-sea transshipment** — allowing vessels to stay at sea for extended periods, obscuring catch origins
- On vessels operating **>1 year without returning to port**, reports of physical abuse and illegal practices increased sharply
- **China's distant-water squid fleet** (the world's largest) was associated with the most severe outcomes across nearly every indicator
- Widespread **shark finning**, capture of vulnerable marine species
- Reports of **violence, wage theft, and deaths at sea**

## Key Quote

> "What this investigation reveals is a systemic failure of governance on the high seas. In the absence of transparency and effective regulation, illegal fishing, environmental destruction and human rights abuses are not exceptions; they are the norm." — Steve Trent, CEO, EJF

## Relevance to Our Data

**Explains the "forced labor encounters" field** in our IUU Risk dataset. GFW's `number_forced_labor_encounters` indicator is derived from an internally maintained list of reported forced-labor vessels — this EJF report is the investigative journalism behind those numbers.

**Connects to our Disabling Map:** The Southwest Atlantic (off Argentina's EEZ, "Mile 201") is one of the top AIS disabling hotspots in our data. EJF confirms Chinese squid vessels increased fishing hours there by **85% between 2019–2024**, even as catch volumes declined — a classic overfishing signal.

**Connects to Sheet 3 (Monthly Trend):** Squid jigging vessels are the most seasonally variable gear type in the GFW data. This report explains why — they follow squid migration patterns across hemispheres.
