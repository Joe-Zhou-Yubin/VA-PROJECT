## Key Facts

- China operates the **world's largest distant-water fishing fleet**
- Government subsidies amount to approximately **US$1.8 billion annually**
- At least **45% of profits** from the Chinese distant-water fleet come from subsidies (Planet Tracker, 2024)
- A **1% increase in fuel subsidy increases fishing hours by 2.2%** (NBER regression discontinuity study)
- Reducing Chinese distant-water fuel subsidies by 50% could **eliminate biological overfishing in several ocean regions**
- China, Taiwan, South Korea, and Spain collectively drove **44% of global fishing effort** between 2022–2024

## China's Summer Fishing Moratorium

- In effect since **1995** (Yellow/East China Sea) and **1999** (South China Sea)
- Runs annually from **May 1 to August 16 / September 1** (varies by sea area)
- Affects hundreds of thousands of coastal fishing vessels
- Bohai/Yellow Sea: May 1 – Sep 1
- East China Sea: May 1 – Aug 16 / Sep 16 (depends on gear)
- South China Sea: May 1 – Aug 16

## WTO Fisheries Subsidies Agreement (June 2022)

- First multilateral deal to curb harmful fishing subsidies
- China recently conceded that some of its vessels do not comply
- Announced it will cancel subsidies to non-compliant distant-water companies
- But: Chinese coastal cities are simultaneously **competing to attract distant-water fishing firms** with generous local subsidies (up to CNY 3M per firm)

## Relevance to Our Data

**Explains the overall upward trend in China's fishing hours (blue line, Sheet 3):** The steady climb from 2020 to 2024 is not organic growth — it's subsidy-driven fleet expansion. Each year, more vessels deploy for longer, pushing total hours up.

**Explains the V-shaped dips every summer:** The moratorium forces the entire coastal fleet to dock. This creates the recurring May–August drops visible in every year of our data.

**Validates China's dominance in our datasets:** China accounts for ~170M fishing hours in 2024 (our data), dwarfing all other nations combined. This is not because Chinese waters are unusually productive — it's because the fleet is subsidized to fish globally at scales no other country can match.

**Relevant for Sheet 17 (Fishing vs. Risk Bubble):** China should appear as the largest bubble (most vessels), with a moderate risk score that belies the scale of concerning behavior when viewed in absolute numbers.
