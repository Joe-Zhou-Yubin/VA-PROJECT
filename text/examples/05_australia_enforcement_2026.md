# Australian Border Force — Illegal Fishing Enforcement 2026

**Source:** https://www.abf.gov.au/newsroom-subsite/Pages/Newsroom.aspx
**Type:** Government enforcement press releases
**Period covered:** January – August 2026

## Key Statistics

- Since 1 January 2026, ABF has intercepted **21 illegal foreign fishing vessels**
- Seized more than **3.5 tonnes of sea cucumber**
- **190 Indonesian fishers** prosecuted in Darwin Local Court since 1 July 2025

## Incident: Three Vessels Apprehended (May 2026)

- **Date:** 1–2 May 2026
- **Location:** North of Cape Wessel, Northern Territory + Mount Adolphus Island, Torres Strait
- Two vessels detected with ~1,000m gill net each, suspected of targeting sharks for fins
- Third vessel concealed within mangroves, believed targeting sea cucumber
- All three vessels **seized and destroyed** under Australian law
- 20 crew members **pleaded guilty** at Darwin Local Court
- Penalties: 3-month imprisonment (repeat offenders) to 2-year good behaviour bonds

## Incident: Two Vessels at Prince of Wales Island (April 2026)

- **Date:** 13 April 2026
- **Location:** Prince of Wales Island, Torres Strait (12 nautical miles from Thursday Island)
- Two Indonesian fishing vessels intercepted
- Evidence: 300kg sea cucumber + 300kg preserving salt + fishing equipment
- Detected through **local community reports** + ABF aerial/surface assets
- Part of **Operation BROADSTAFF** — multi-agency operation targeting illegal foreign fishing

## Quotes

> "Illegal fishing in Australian waters carries significant risks. Foreign nationals caught fishing unlawfully face severe penalties, including confiscation of their catch, equipment and vessel, as well as possible imprisonment." — AFMA General Manager Justin Bathurst

> "Anyone who attempts to fish illegally in Australian waters – you will be found, you will be stopped, and you will face enforcement action under Australian law." — Acting Deputy Commander Brooke Dewar, Maritime Border Command

## Relevance to Our Data

**Connects to our disabling hotspot near Northern Australia:** The Torres Strait and northern Australian waters are areas where AIS disabling is expected — small Indonesian vessels often don't carry AIS at all, and those that do may disable it when entering Australian waters illegally.

**Explains the "UNKNOWN" and "IDN" flag entries** in our disabling data — many apprehended vessels are small Indonesian craft operating without proper documentation.

**Demonstrates enforcement response patterns:** Australia uses a "layered approach" — air, sea, land patrols + community intelligence. This is the kind of enforcement our dashboard could help support through AIS monitoring.
