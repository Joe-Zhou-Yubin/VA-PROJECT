# The Threat of Overfishing on Biodiversity and the Environment: An Examination of Global Efforts and the Need for Continued Action

## ABSTRACT

Overfishing is an urgent problem that needs to be resolved. While there have been measures to tackle overfishing such as having marine protected areas and imposing limits on fishing, the efficiency of these measures is disputable. Hence, this project aims to provide data driven suggestions on what more can be done to address overfishing to protect our marine biodiversity and environment through:

- **(a)** Understanding the current state of seafood & fish production and consumption
- **(b)** Analyzing the impact of overfishing
- **(c)** Assessing existing global efforts to mitigate overfishing – its limitations and opportunities

## PROBLEM STATEMENT AND MOTIVATION

Overfishing poses a significant threat to food security. Globally, over 3 billion people depend heavily on fish as their main source of protein (Future of Fish, n.d.). As overfishing destroys marine life (including fish supply), this leads to a shortage of food in underdeveloped countries where switching to alternatives is difficult due to access and financial constraints (Leschin-Hoar, 2016).

Additionally, overfishing harms the environment. It reduces the ability of the ocean, the largest carbon sink in the world, to effectively absorb carbon dioxide from the atmosphere (United Nations Climate Change, 2022). This means that more carbon dioxide, a greenhouse gas, gets trapped in the air accelerating global warming. Global warming in turn puts us at risk when temperatures become too hot to survive.

Threatened food security and climate change are just two consequences of overfishing. Given the severity and the widespread impact of overfishing, it is thus crucial that this issue is addressed.
